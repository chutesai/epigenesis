\begin{abstract}
An epigenetic language model keeps its base weights as a frozen genome and writes acquired knowledge
into a separate sparse layer occupying exact zeros. Removing that layer restores the base bit-for-bit.
FSA targets the pair8 forced-zero mask; serving that design needs a sparse overlay or wider block encoding and remains pending. We test the in-pair variant on a real 8B ternary mixture-of-experts model. On 40 facts,
ternary Bop with a local output penalty, a 300k-slot budget and rehearsal recalls 79--80/80 held-out
items under teacher forcing and greedy generation across three seeds with different fact values: mean 99.2\% on both metrics,
with mean general-text $\Delta$NLL $-0.0003$ (range $-0.0015$ to $+0.0006$). It fills 245k--256k
slots, adding no parameters and preserving the packed format; position-inclusive storage is
0.32--0.86 MB. All three seeds revoke bit-exactly. LoRA baselines recall 75--78/80 but remain single
seed, so their run-to-run spread is unmeasured; 1--2 item gaps are within noise, while 3--4 may matter. Rehearsal and damage evaluation use disjoint
sequences from the same corpus. Synthetic in-pair packed patches show no detectable serving slowdown;
learned patches are not yet benchmarked. At 300 conflicting facts, FSA reaches only 18.2\% recall
and fits 22.9\% of training facts, versus LoRA's 63.0\% TF with fp32 factors and 64.8\% at int4.
The LoRA factors quantize to int4 without loss on this task. EP-1 v1's five-session self-distillation failed the concept-gain criterion in every
arm, including LoRA, but its teacher was later found not to know the probed rules, so v1 was not a valid test.
Capacity and multi-session consolidation remain open; EP-1 v2.1 is being designed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
We use an epigenetic analogy: the base weights are a frozen genome, while acquired knowledge lives in
a separate sparse layer that can be removed to recover the base bit-for-bit. Extreme quantization
already leaves many weights at exact zero. Can those existing slots carry new knowledge without
adding parameters?

\textbf{Free-Slot Adaptation (FSA).} In the pair8 ternary format, at most two of every four aligned
pairs are active. The design's write set is the two unselected pairs: 4 of every 8 weights
(at least half). This deterministic forced-zero mask ships with the model. Let $\Sz$ be the
base's nonzero support and $\Zz$ its zeros. Freeze the base and shared parameters, then train
$\Rd$ only within the forced-zero mask. Store filled positions and signs as a removable patch.
Enabling it uses $\Sz\cup\Rd$; zeroing the mask restores $\Sz$ and the exact pair8 base.
Filling unselected pairs can exceed pair8's two-pair limit. Serving needs a sparse overlay beside
the packed kernel or blocks re-encoded in a wider format; this path is not built or benchmarked.
All existing FSA results instead use the in-pair variant, restricted to zeros inside
already-active pairs. This is \citet{packnet}'s mechanism, with the reserve supplied by the
compression format rather than a pruning pass.

\textbf{What we claim.} On 40 facts, the headline recipe recalls 79--80/80 TF and GEN across
three seeds with different fact values, using 245k--256k slots (0.32--0.86 MB including positions), with damage within noise of
zero and bit-exact revoke. The nearest-size LoRA, rank 1 int4 (0.69 MB), recalls 75/80 on both
metrics. Gaps of 1--2 items are within noise; 3--4 may matter, but all LoRA baselines remain single seed; their
run-to-run spread is unmeasured. No damage difference is detectable between the best penalized
FSA runs and fp32 penalized LoRA. Synthetic in-pair packed patches have no detectable serving cost;
learned patches still need benchmarking. At 300 conflicting facts the FSA recipe fails to fit its
training facts and loses to LoRA. EP-1 v1 failed concept gain for every arm. These results do not
establish lifelong learning or general personalization.

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) Quantization zeros as a removable adaptation reserve, with accounting for
positions, values, scales, and inference format. (2) Real-model results comparing ternary Bop,
learn-then-prune, fp4, and LoRA on recall, damage, and storage. (3) A measured KL floor under random
perturbations, with routing near-ties as a possible explanation rather than an established cause.
(4) A patch-revocation contract and a pre-registered multi-session concept experiment
(\S\ref{sec:direction}).

\section{Background and related work}
\textbf{Mask-based continual learning.} PackNet \citep{packnet} freezes kept weights and trains freed ones,
recovering a task by its mask; Piggyback \citep{piggyback} \emph{learns masks over fixed weights} (it does
not refill zeros); HAT \citep{hat} uses hard task-attention and reports \emph{reduced}, not zero,
forgetting; Supermasks \citep{supermasks} make the mask itself the trainable object; Progressive Networks
\citep{progressivenn} add and could drop columns. FSA is PackNet with the free support supplied by the
quantization format. \textbf{Quantized continual learning.} Ada-QPackNet \citep{adaqpacknet} and
TinySubNets \citep{tinysubnets} already combine PackNet-style capacity reuse with quantization; SPDF
\citep{spdf} reopens zeroed pretrained weights for dense fine-tuning. These sharply narrow our novelty; our
distinct element is a reserve \emph{pre-addressed by the deployed format} and composed by union.
\textbf{Parameter-efficient and continual adapters.} LoRA \citep{lora}, QLoRA \citep{qlora}, QA-LoRA
\citep{qalora}; \citet{loralearnsless} find LoRA learns and forgets less than full fine-tuning (a possible
rank explanation, not a theorem); O-LoRA \citep{olora} does continual learning in orthogonal LoRA
subspaces. \textbf{Sparse / composable adaptation.} Diff pruning \citep{diffpruning} and composable sparse
fine-tuning \citep{composablesft}; union of \emph{disjoint} sparse supports is the easy case of the latter.
\textbf{Editing, memory, unlearning.} MEMIT \citep{memit} edits facts in place and can override; FSA adds paths that can
suppress or correct outputs while enabled but cannot edit a base weight; GRACE \citep{grace} and WISE \citep{wise} keep deletable, routed side-memory for sequential edits
while freezing the base; SISA \citep{sisa} is exact unlearning by shard isolation; TOFU \citep{tofu}
benchmarks fictitious-fact forgetting. \textbf{Personalization and retrieval.} OPPU \citep{oppu} attaches a
per-user PEFT module (removed by user identity, not per record); LaMP \citep{lamp} is a personalization
benchmark with retrieval baselines; RAG \citep{rag} and kNN-LM \citep{knnlm} store facts externally and
delete them by dropping a record---the baseline any ``delete a fact'' claim must beat.
\textbf{Low-bit models.} BitNet b1.58 \citep{bitnet158,bitnet2b4t} and LittleBit \citep{littlebit}. Bop \citep{bop} optimizes binary
weights without latent real weights; we extend its gradient-memory rule to ternary birth and death
(\S\ref{sec:writers}).

\section{Method}
\subsection{Format and reserve}
In \texttt{pair8}, weights form aligned blocks of eight (four pairs); each weight is ternary times a scale,
and at most two pairs per block are non-zero. A block thus has $417$ reachable states
($1 + 4\!\cdot\!8 + \binom{4}{2}8^2$), i.e. $\log_2 417/8 \approx 1.088$ bits/weight asymptotically
($1.125$ at a fixed $9$ bits/block), and is $\ge 50\%$ zero \emph{by construction} (ternary alone
guarantees no such fraction). \textbf{Design reserve.} The forced-zero mask selects the two pairs
pair8 did not select: 4 of every 8 weights (at least half). It is deterministic and ships with the
model. A patch writes only there. Filling those pairs can produce more than two nonzero pairs,
which is illegal pair8. Serving requires a sparse overlay beside the packed kernel or wider block
encoding; implementation and measurements are pending.

\textbf{Measured variant.} All reported FSA runs use in-pair slots: zeros inside already-active
pairs, a different set of base zeros from the forced-zero mask (the two sets are disjoint). Filling them preserves pair occupancy;
plain ternary values also preserve the codebook. Fp4 and learned scales do not. In-pair slots and
forced-zero slots lie in different pairs. Filled positions and signs must still be stored for
revocation; the joint rate of 1.088 bits/weight is not the marginal price of a filled slot.

\subsection{Adaptation and exact revoke}
Train only the free-slot patch $\Delta W$, with support $\Rd\cap\Sz=\varnothing$, while the base
remains live. Base weights, per-row scales, router, and norms are frozen. The in-pair variant also
freezes pair occupancy. A ternary writer fills masked zero slots with $\pm\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the frozen row scale. The enabled
model uses $W+\Delta W$; zeroing the filled slots returns every patched tensor bit-identical to $W$,
since the patch never overwrites base support or shared state. In the 40-fact runs, patch-off logits were
bitwise equal to the base on one 512-token held-out window, and test recall returned to 0/80.
The 300-fact run returned to 0/600; EP-1 revoke restored its own baseline metrics.

This restores the base, without guaranteeing its behavior while the patch is on. It does not delete
individual facts within a patch. If later sessions train with an earlier patch enabled, zeroing that
session's slots alone need not remove its influence on retained patches or training state
(\S\ref{sec:direction}).

\subsection{Honest accounting}
FSA fills existing zero weights without changing the logical element count. Only the plain ternary
in-pair variant uses the same packed tensors and kernel with no added tensors or inference compute.
Synthetic in-pair patches preserve file size and memory at reported precision and revoke to a
byte-identical packed file (\S\ref{sec:serving}); learned patches have not been benchmarked.
The forced-zero design needs a sparse overlay or wider block encoding; its serving cost, file size
and memory are pending. Zeroing its mask still restores the exact base. Storage is not free: revocation needs positions and signs in a stored
diff, or the original pack. The learned-block-scale arm adds parameters and storage and is outside these packed-format claims. A full per-domain mask would cost one bit
per weight; sparse position coding avoids that cost for sparse patches. Frozen row scales constrain
each ternary slot to $\{-\alpha,0,+\alpha\}$, a capacity restriction. Actual position-inclusive patch
sizes are reported in \S\ref{sec:results}; hypothetical trit-only accounting is insufficient.

\section{Real-model setup}
\label{sec:real}
Lambda is an 8B-parameter ternary MoE with 128 routed experts per MoE
layer, 12 active per token, model width 1152, and a recurrent sequence-mixing state between layers.
Each routed expert has a $2304\!\times\!384$ up projection, squared ReLU, and a
$384\!\times\!2304$ down projection, stored as \texttt{pair8} trits times frozen per-row scales.
All reported FSA runs use the in-pair variant, a different set of base zeros (inside the selected pairs, disjoint from the forced-zero mask) that keeps pair occupancy legal; plain ternary patches keep pair8 legal. Layers 14--15, all 128 experts and both projections provide 66,807,179 in-pair slots. Forced-zero-mask results are pending. Below, ``slots'' denotes the in-pair write set. Historical late-layer ablations use the same in-pair rule at their stated placement. Base support, scales, pair occupancy, router, and all other parameters
remain frozen. Plain ternary births take value $\pm\alpha$ for row scale $\alpha$. The block-scale
arm keeps ternary signs but multiplies them by learned magnitudes, one per row and 16 input columns,
initialized at $0.5\alpha$. Its values leave $\{-\alpha,0,+\alpha\}$; the packed-format and
no-new-parameter claims do not cover this arm. The fp4 variant uses magnitudes
$\{0,0.125,0.25,0.375,0.5,0.75,1,1.5\}\alpha$ (the E2M1 grid scaled by $0.25\alpha$),
with minimum nonzero magnitude $0.125\alpha$ and cap $1.5\alpha$; it leaves the ternary codebook. Evaluation decodes experts to bf16. Plain ternary in-pair patches could use the same packed
kernel; synthetic patches have been benchmarked there (\S\ref{sec:serving}), while accuracy
evaluation still used decoded bf16 weights.

The task has 40 synthetic facts about a fictional person (favorite cheese, boat's name, house number).
Training lasts 300 steps with answer-token-only loss. Each step samples three phrasings per fact from
a pool of 12 templates (three plain sentences and nine augmented forms, including
``Question:/Answer:'' and ``User:/Assistant:''). Two further phrasings and $16\!\times\!512$ general-text
windows provide validation monitoring and checkpoint selection. Test recall is scored under teacher forcing: given the prompt, the model's greedy (argmax) prediction
must match every answer token. The new runs also measure free-running greedy generated recall:
the whole answer must match at a word boundary, then a terminator or end-of-sequence within
two tokens (text after that is ignored). Older runs test teacher-forced recall only. Recall is measured
on two phrasings never used in training or validation: ``Q: What is X's attr? A:'' and
``A little-known fact about X: her attr is''. The Q/A form is held out verbatim but resembles trained
question/answer formats. There are 80 test items; one item is 1.25 percentage points, and the unpatched
model gets 0/80. Damage is mean per-token $\Delta$NLL relative to the base on 40 disjoint 512-token
WikiText windows (about 20k tokens), with paired standard error (SE) over windows. The unpatched
model's NLL is 2.890 on a separate small diagnostic pool of five 512-token windows,
which also provides the reference for the chaos-floor NLL ranges. All evaluations run one sequence
at a time: 16-prompt batching flipped 15/32 greedy base continuations, so 1--2 item gaps are within noise.
The legacy $5\!\times\!512$ KL/top-1 set shares 57\% of its 32-token sequences with training anchors;
the $40\!\times\!512$ $\Delta$NLL panel, validation and conversation text have zero overlap, enforced by the harness.
Each of the three headline seeds varies both fact values and training randomness: seed 1 differs from seed 0 on
33/40 attribute values, and seed 2 on 34/40. Other runs, including LoRA, use one each. Same-seed repeats
do not test robustness across seeds. Table~\ref{tab:real} reports step 300, except repair,
which adds 50 steps with births disabled.

\section{A chaos floor for KL}
Random, untrained ternary flips produce a KL floor while NLL on the separate five-window diagnostic
pool stays within about 0.01 nats of the unpatched model. For the down-projection flips in
Table~\ref{tab:chaos} and Figure~\ref{fig:chaos}, the range is about $-0.010$ to $+0.003$ relative to
the diagnostic-pool base NLL of 2.890; across all flip settings tested, it is about $-0.010$ to $+0.005$. A single layer-15 zero in one expert's down projection
illustrates the bf16 threshold: changes at most $0.01\alpha$ round away; a full trit changes about 6\%
of top-1 tokens. Two different single flips disagree with each other by KL 0.027, slightly more than one flip
differs from the base (0.024), and the divergence is roughly flat across sequence position. These observations suggest a different
but equally good trajectory after a change exceeds bf16 resolution. Near-ties in discrete MoE routing,
propagated through recurrent state, are a plausible driver, not a proven explanation.

For always-on edits the typical KL floor is about 0.02--0.027, with layer-dependent exceptions in
Table~\ref{tab:chaos}. A KL penalty mostly fights this floor: increasing its weight from 1 to 30 left
KL near 0.06 while recall fell from 48\% to 20\%. We therefore measure damage with paired $\Delta$NLL,
interpret KL relative to this floor, and use a local output penalty for preservation (\S\ref{sec:writers}).

ranks 1--4 and measured precisions, at 0.69--11.01 MB (int4 to bf16). The nearest LoRA size is

The best penalized FSA runs, with or without rehearsal, and fp32 penalized LoRA all have

On the 40-fact in-conversation test, the question follows about 545 tokens of unrelated chat.
The headline achieves 85\% generated recall versus 40\% for the unbudgeted base (single seed,
40 items). Short-prompt recall does not distinguish them beyond noise.

\section{Writers}
\label{sec:writers}
\subsection{Ternary Bop} Bop is Helwegen et al.'s binary optimizer \citep{bop}. Our ternary
extension adds the birth and death rules below and resets the gradient EMA after every change.
Each in-pair slot holds
$q_j\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ directly, with no latent real weight. For loss gradient $g_j$, maintain
\[
m_j\leftarrow(1-\gamma)m_j+\gamma g_j,\qquad \gamma=0.05.
\]
If $q_j=0$ and $|m_j|>\tau$, birth sets $q_j\leftarrow-\operatorname{sign}(m_j)$.
If $q_j\ne0$, $|m_j|>\tau$, and $\operatorname{sign}(m_j)=q_j$, death sets $q_j\leftarrow0$:
the gradient says the current sign hurts. Reset $m_j$ to zero after each change. Thresholds are 0.7 or
0.9 times the RMS of the first answer-loss gradient over in-pair slots. Historical plain Bop runs use
no penalty; new runs add the local penalty below. Bop does not use weight decay. The
threshold controls flips: small gradients need not become full $\pm\alpha$ steps, unlike Adam's
per-coordinate normalization, which can turn persistent small gradients into full flips.
In the historical single-seed runs, slots are filled early: 812,077 slots were filled at step 50 (812,420 at step 300) at $\tau=0.7$. Higher $\tau$ uses fewer
slots (589k) at slightly higher damage, so it is not a simple damage dial. The $\tau=0.7$
repeat has 669,465 filled slots at step 50 and 669,705 at step 300; its revoke is also bit-exact,
with test recall returning to 0/80.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../figures/fig_e2e_trajectory.pdf}
\caption{Validation recall, validation $\Delta$NLL (symmetric log scale), and filled slots (millions,
log scale) across training. The recall and damage panels show learn-then-prune, ternary Bop $\tau=0.7$
run 1, and regularized LoRA; learn-then-prune uses decay 0.3. The filled-slot panel shows
only those two FSA writers; the new repeat and decay 0.5 trajectories are not plotted.
Bop's filled-slot count is set by its threshold early and then stays flat, while learn-then-prune

\label{fig:trajectory}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Learn-then-prune} These historical comparisons use a single seed per run. Each in-pair slot has an fp32 master $w_j$ in row-scale units. Forward uses
$q_j=\operatorname{sign}(w_j)\mathrm{I}(|w_j|>0.5)$, where $\mathrm{I}$ is the indicator; gradients pass straight through
\citep{ste}. AdamW \citep{adamw}, learning rate 0.02, applies decoupled weight decay 0.3 to masters.
The model learns early: at step 50 validation recall is 97.5\%, with 20.3M filled slots and validation
$\Delta$NLL $+2.2$. Decay pulls weakly supported masters under threshold: 3.2M slots at step 100,
1.1M at 150, and 0.54M at 300. Validation damage falls to about $+0.02$ and validation recall stays
100\%; test recall is separately 91.25\%. Held-out damage fell from $+0.0282$ at step 250 to
$+0.0250$ at step 300, while validation damage rose slightly ($+0.016 \to +0.018$).
With decay 0.5, filled slots fall from 14.18M at step 50 (98.75\% validation recall)
to 1.12M at step 100, 0.62M at 150, 0.50M at 200, and 0.45M at 250 (92.5\% validation recall),
then rise to 0.52M at 300 (97.5\% validation recall). Test recall at step 300 is 88.75\%;
revoke is bit-exact. Whether longer pruning helps is untested.
\par

\subsection{Fp4 and the local output penalty} Four-bit float slots times $\alpha$ use AdamW decay 0.1
and add the general-text penalty
\[
\lambda\frac{\sum_{l,t}\|x_t\Delta W_l^T\|^2}{\sum_{l,t}\|x_tW_l^T\|^2+10^{-12}},\qquad\lambda=10,
\]
where $l$ ranges over patched linear projections, $t$ over general-text tokens, $x_t$ is the input to
that projection, $W_l$ is its frozen base weight, and $\Delta W_l$ is the patch. The denominator,
base-weight output energy on the current inputs, is treated as a constant normalizer and is not
differentiated. This smooth local
penalty measures each patched projection's direct output change rather than the change in final
logits, so it is not dominated by the whole-model divergence behind the KL floor. Its inputs still come from the patched forward
pass: earlier patched layers can change later inputs and routing. The best LoRA run uses the same penalty
with $\lambda=100$. At that strength the penalty stopped earlier ternary learning: its gradient
is zero at zero, yet one birth costs a full $\alpha^2\mathbb{E}[x^2]$. Bop with $\lambda=10$
now learns while keeping measured damage within noise of zero (Table~\ref{tab:real}).
\par


\subsection{Budget and preservation variants}
\textbf{Gain-minus-cost budget.} At most 300k slots may be filled. The allowed set
is re-chosen every 10 steps up to step 200 (the harness default; that setting is not stored in the
result files), by each slot's gradient evidence minus its expected general-text cost,
$\alpha^2$ times the input column's general-text energy. Births go where evidence for the facts
is strongest relative to expected general-text change.
\textbf{Rehearsal.} The headline objective is $0.8$ times fact loss plus $0.2$ times ordinary
next-token loss on general WikiText windows, alongside the local penalty. Those windows share
no 32-token sequences with the damage panel but come from the same corpus. Near-zero damage is
therefore measured in-distribution; other kinds of text remain untested.
\textbf{Energy-scaled threshold.} The row's birth threshold is multiplied by
$\operatorname{clamp}((e_{\rm row}/\operatorname{median}e)^{0.5},0.25,4)$, where $e_{\rm row}$
is the expected general-text output energy of one birth in that row and the median is over rows with in-pair slots and positive energy. Sensitive rows need more evidence.
\textbf{Repair.} After 300 training steps, 50 more Bop steps keep the local penalty at $\lambda=10$
and disable births, allowing deaths only. Rehearsal has three seeds with different fact values; other variants have one each.
Recall differences of 1--2 items are within noise and do not establish their relative value.

\subsection{Ingredients that mattered} Answer-only loss avoids learning template boilerplate, which
caused most early damage with whole-sentence loss. Twelve diverse formats, including Q/A, largely
closed the validation-to-test gap (Bop and penalized LoRA test at their validation recall;
learn-then-prune decay 0.3 remains 100\% validation vs 91.25\% test). An earlier protocol reached
only 54\%/56\% held-out recall. The broad footprint mattered: 32 selected experts in one layer, down only, with a 500k-slot budget (350k--404k slots filled)
starved capacity (19--29\% recall). Damage uses the roughly 20k-token held-out pool, with checkpoint selection on a
separate validation pool.

\subsection{What did not work} KL penalties fight the chaos floor and cost recall. Ternary slots with
smooth penalty and decay peak in recall, then are pruned away. Tiny fp4 slots in late layer 28,
down only, with a 2M-slot budget and grid magnitudes starting at $0.03125\alpha$, fill about 2.0M
slots but yield 0\% recall at $+0.0067$ damage: the patch changed the model but learned none of
the tested answers. Patch-only learned block scales are no better than plain ternary
(87.5\%/$+0.028$) and add substantial storage. Single-late-layer continuous bf16 slots give
48.75\%/$+0.047$, versus same-placement LoRA's 37.5\%/$+0.0014$: a recall--damage trade, not an
overall improvement over LoRA. Gain-minus-cost ternary slot selection at that placement gives
60\%/$+0.064$.

Churn controls also failed to improve on the headline. Against the unbudgeted, unrehearsed
Bop $\tau=0.7$ + penalty 10 base (78/80 TF and GEN, $-0.0011\pm0.0019$, 735,955 slots),
preventing death for 20 steps after birth gives 80/80 on both metrics, $+0.0024\pm0.0017$,
and 543,145 slots. A 50-step grace gives 79/80, $+0.0052\pm0.0017$, and 769,165 slots;
requiring 1.5 times the birth threshold for death gives 79/80, $+0.0029\pm0.0019$, and
783,165 slots. Recall differs by only 1--2 items, within noise, while damage is slightly higher than the base;
grace 50 is about three SE above zero. None is an improvement.

Post-training utility pruning ranked slots by a first-order estimate and tried 10\%, then 5\%,
then 2.5\% chunks, accepting only if validation recall held. The headline accepted no chunk:
0\% removed from 249,755 slots. Removing the ``least useful'' 10\% raised fact loss despite a
predicted decrease, and validation TF recall fell to 81\%. The unbudgeted base accepted one
2.5\% chunk (717,557 slots kept), but test TF fell from 78/80 to 75/80 and GEN from 78/80
to 77/80 (the one-item GEN drop is within noise). The first-order estimate is wrong at a trained
minimum (fact loss $\approx0.01$): gradients are near zero, while reverting a flip is a full-$\alpha$
step. Reverting the predicted least-useful 10\% raised base fact loss by $+0.258$ versus a predicted
$-0.025$; for the headline, $+0.150$ versus $-0.013$. Just 7.5\% of the base patch's flips carry
half the positive first-order utility. The failure diagnoses the estimate, rather than proving
there is no removable slack. Exact leave-out by expert and by layer is running.

\section{Toward epigenesis: multi-session learning}
\label{sec:direction}
The in-pair ternary reserve now carries facts on a real model (\S\ref{sec:real}). A revocable domain patch
is therefore demonstrated for this task: enable the slice for its domain; disable it to recover the base.
The personal-model application remains an untested proposal, with a narrower contract:
\par

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Patch-revocation contract.} Deleting an isolated patch exactly removes \emph{that patch's parameter
contribution}. Equivalence to a model trained without its data additionally requires that no retained patch
or training state depended on it. It does not delete individual facts (facts within a slice are
superimposed), and it does not make information already in the base inaccessible.
\end{quote}

Retrieval deletes individual records while leaving the base unchanged \citep{rag,knnlm}; disabling a
per-user adapter removes that user's contribution \citep{oppu}. The case for weights is skills, style,
and policy that benefit from adaptation in every forward pass. Arbitrary facts remain a retrieval
baseline's natural task. The real ternary fact experiment offers smaller patches, with no detectable damage difference
between the best penalized FSA runs and fp32 penalized LoRA at this sample size.
General personalization remains untested.

EP-1 v1 uses the in-pair write set; its fp4 and learned-scale arms leave the original codebook.
It completed five sessions, distilling each session's self-study into a patch. The synthetic
user contains 40 policies, 100 recurrent facts (each in three sessions), and 100 one-off lure facts
(20 per session). On 100 four-way test concept items, the unpatched model scores 0.24 (chance 0.25).

Fp4 slots & 0.32 & +8 points\\
Ternary Bop (added after pre-registration) & 0.32 & +8 points\\

The thesis required at least +10 concept accuracy points over the base after session 5, with lure,
damage, retention and revoke criteria. Every arm, including LoRA, failed concept gain; all other
criteria passed. Lure recall is 0/50 in every arm, with no upward ratchet. Damage bounds and
retention passed; revoke restored bitwise weights, legal format and identical logits, and cleared
operational state. Session-5 damage is within noise of zero for the ternary arms; fp4 is
$+0.0074\pm0.0020$. The pre-registered ternary arm learns block scales and is outside the original
packed-format and no-new-parameter claims; fp4 also leaves the ternary codebook.

The in-context ceiling measured in v1 (1.00) was invalid: each probe's context held only the correct
policy's source episode, so it contained the answer. With all four rules of the category in context, the
unpatched model picks the right action only 0.30 of the time (ticket 0.27, Q/A 0.34; chance 0.25). The
identifiability gate was therefore invalid and the self-distillation teacher was never competent on the
probed skill, so v1 cannot test whether slots consolidate concepts. EP-1 v2.1 uses coarser categorical
rules and a full-table context, or a ground-truth supervised target if the teacher still fails; the
multi-session thesis remains open. The 300-fact
failure also calls for budget-scaled reruns and better optimization. LoRA seed spread, learned-patch
serving and broader preservation need measurement. Per the pre-registration, one seed is a pilot;
three are needed to claim. On-device training is not established: optimizer state can be large even
when a final patch is small. A broader evaluation should include local retrieval and per-user
adapters on personalization benchmarks \citep{lamp}, with unlearning measured \citep{sisa,tofu}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Damage and recall.} The best penalized FSA runs and fp32 penalized LoRA are within noise of
zero on 40 WikiText windows, without a detectable damage difference at this sample size.
Plain Bop costs $+0.024$ to $+0.031$. Damage on other corpora and broader behavior is untested;
rehearsal windows are sequence-disjoint but drawn from the same corpus as the damage panel.
Generated recall is now measured for the new runs, checking unaided answer production and a clean
stop; older runs remain teacher-forced only. Eval batch composition changes this model's outputs
(16-prompt batching flipped 15/32 greedy base continuations); all evaluations run one sequence at
a time, and 1--2 item recall gaps are within noise. The legacy $5\!\times\!512$ KL/top-1 set
overlaps training anchors (57\% of 32-token sequences), so it is not held out for penalty runs.
The $40\!\times\!512$ $\Delta$NLL panel, validation and conversation text have zero overlap,
enforced by the harness.

\textbf{Evaluation scope and capacity.} The 40-fact headline has three seeds with different fact values, 79--80/80 TF and GEN,
with damage within noise of zero; other runs, including LoRA, have one seed each. Gaps of 1--2 items
are within noise; 3--4 may matter, but LoRA's run-to-run spread is unmeasured. The Q/A test form resembles training;
writer and regularization choices differ. At 300 conflicting facts, FSA reaches only 18.2\% recall
and fits 22.9\% of training facts, versus LoRA's 63.0\% TF fp32 / 64.8\% int4. This
capacity or optimization failure limits the current recipe; FSA reruns with 2M- and 4M-slot budgets
at 1,200 steps are running, with results pending.
EP-1 v1 failed concept gain for every arm, including LoRA; v2 is being designed. The chaos-floor
explanation is a hypothesis, and the roughly 20k-token damage pool cannot establish broad behavioral safety.

\textbf{Revoke scope.} Removing the whole patch restores the base bit-for-bit. This does not guarantee
preservation while the patch is on, deletion of individual facts, removal of information already in
the base, or removal of an earlier session's influence after later sessions trained on top of it.
The frozen base can be augmented or suppressed through new paths but its existing weights cannot
be refined.

\textbf{Format and deployment.} Plain ternary in-pair writers stay legal pair8, but evaluation decoded experts
to bf16; packed-kernel serving is measured for synthetic legal patches only, with learned patches
not yet benchmarked. Fp4 and learned-block-scale variants leave the
original codebook. Forced-zero serving needs a sparse overlay or wider block encoding; serving cost,
file size and memory are pending. Optimizer state can be large even when the final patch is small, and on-device
training is not established.

\textbf{Privacy.} Knowledge in weights can be elicited. A per-user model is the proposed deployment for the personal application; its safety is untested, and
the patch mechanism supplies no privacy guarantee.

\section{Conclusion}
FSA targets the deterministic pair8 forced-zero mask; zeroing it restores the exact base. Its
sparse-overlay or wider-block serving path is not built or benchmarked; serving cost, file size
and memory are pending. All reported FSA results use the in-pair variant. Only plain
ternary in-pair patches retain the same packed kernel; unchanged file size and memory and
byte-identical packed-file revoke were measured on synthetic in-pair patches. On a real 8B ternary
MoE, Bop with a local penalty, a slot budget and rehearsal recalls 79--80/80 TF and GEN across three
seeds with different fact values: mean 99.2\% (range 98.75--100\%), mean $\Delta$NLL $-0.0003$ (range $-0.0015$ to
$+0.0006$), and 245k--256k slots (0.32--0.86 MB including positions). All three revoke bit-exactly.
LoRA baselines remain single seed, leaving their run-to-run spread unmeasured. No damage difference
is detectable between the best penalized FSA runs and fp32 penalized LoRA at this sample size,
on the same corpus used for rehearsal. Synthetic in-pair packed patches have no detectable serving cost;
learned-patch serving and broader preservation remain untested.

The recipe fails at 300 conflicting facts: 18.2\% recall and only 22.9\% training-fact recall,
versus LoRA's 63.0\% TF fp32 / 64.8\% int4. EP-1 v1 failed the pre-registered concept-gain
criterion for every arm, including LoRA. Multi-session consolidation remains open; v2 is being
designed, alongside running 2M- and 4M-slot-budget fact reruns at 1,200 steps (results pending) and LoRA seeds. These results establish small,
revocable fact patches under the measured protocol, not the lifelong-learning thesis.
