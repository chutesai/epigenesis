\begin{abstract}
An epigenetic language model keeps its base weights as a frozen genome and writes acquired knowledge
into a separate sparse layer occupying exact zeros. Removing that layer restores the base bit-for-bit.
We test Free-Slot Adaptation (FSA) on a real 8B ternary mixture-of-experts model. On one 40-fact task,
ternary Bop with a local output penalty, a 300k-slot budget and rehearsal recalls 79/80 held-out items
under both teacher forcing and free-running greedy generation with a clean stop. It fills 249,755 slots,
adding no parameters and preserving the packed format, at $-0.0001\pm0.0017$ nats/token general-text
change. Position-inclusive storage is 0.33 MB entropy-coded or 0.84 MB indexed; the closest LoRA
baseline, rank 1 with measured int4 factors, costs 0.69 MB and recalls 75/80 on both metrics.
Across tested LoRA ranks and precisions, the recall gap is 1--4 items on one seed: suggestive,
not established; seeds pending. No damage difference is detectable between the best penalized FSA
runs and fp32 penalized LoRA at this sample size. Rehearsal and damage evaluation use disjoint
sequences from the same corpus, so preservation is measured in-distribution. Every run revokes
bit-exactly on the checked window. Synthetic packed patches show no detectable serving slowdown;
learned patches are not yet benchmarked. FSA does not beat LoRA overall on this evidence.
Multi-session concept learning remains untested.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
We use an epigenetic analogy: the base weights are a frozen genome, while acquired knowledge lives in
a separate sparse layer that can be removed to recover the base bit-for-bit. Extreme quantization
already leaves many weights at exact zero. Can those existing slots carry new knowledge without
adding parameters?

\textbf{Free-Slot Adaptation (FSA).} In the pair8 ternary format, at most two of every four aligned
pairs are active. Let $\Sz$ be the base's nonzero support and $\Zz$ its zeros. Freeze the base and all
shared parameters, then train a disjoint slice $\Rd\subseteq\Zz$ inside already-active pairs. Store its
positions and signs as a removable patch. Enabling the patch uses $\Sz\cup\Rd$; disabling it uses
$\Sz$ alone. This is \citet{packnet}'s mechanism, with the reserve supplied by the compression format
rather than a pruning pass.

\textbf{What we claim.} The best observed patch recalls 79/80 teacher-forced and generated items
from 249,755 slots (0.33--0.84 MB including positions), with damage within noise of zero.
The nearest-size LoRA, rank 1 int4 (0.69 MB), recalls 75/80 on both metrics. A 1--4-item gap across
LoRA ranks and measured precisions on one seed is suggestive, not established; seeds pending.
No damage difference is detectable between the best penalized FSA runs and fp32 penalized LoRA.
Synthetic packed patches have no detectable serving cost, but learned patches still need benchmarking.
FSA does not beat LoRA overall; restoration when the patch is removed does not preserve behavior
while it is enabled. Multi-session concept learning and the personal-model application remain untested.

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) Quantization zeros as a removable adaptation reserve, with accounting for
positions, values, scales, and inference format. (2) Real-model results comparing ternary Bop,
learn-then-prune, fp4, and LoRA on recall, damage, and storage. (3) A measured KL floor under random
perturbations, with routing near-ties as a possible explanation rather than an established cause.
(4) A patch-revocation contract and a pre-registered multi-session concept experiment
(\S\ref{sec:direction}).

\section{Background and related work}
\textbf{Mask-based continual learning.} PackNet \citep{packnet} freezes kept weights and trains freed ones,
recovering a task by its mask; Piggyback \citep{piggyback} \emph{learns masks over fixed weights} (it does
not refill zeros); HAT \citep{hat} uses hard task-attention and reports \emph{reduced}, not zero,
forgetting; Supermasks \citep{supermasks} make the mask itself the trainable object; Progressive Networks
\citep{progressivenn} add and could drop columns. FSA is PackNet with the free support supplied by the
quantization format. \textbf{Quantized continual learning.} Ada-QPackNet \citep{adaqpacknet} and
TinySubNets \citep{tinysubnets} already combine PackNet-style capacity reuse with quantization; SPDF
\citep{spdf} reopens zeroed pretrained weights for dense fine-tuning. These sharply narrow our novelty; our
distinct element is a reserve \emph{pre-addressed by the deployed format} and composed by union.
\textbf{Parameter-efficient and continual adapters.} LoRA \citep{lora}, QLoRA \citep{qlora}, QA-LoRA
\citep{qalora}; \citet{loralearnsless} find LoRA learns and forgets less than full fine-tuning (a possible
rank explanation, not a theorem); O-LoRA \citep{olora} does continual learning in orthogonal LoRA
subspaces. \textbf{Sparse / composable adaptation.} Diff pruning \citep{diffpruning} and composable sparse
fine-tuning \citep{composablesft}; union of \emph{disjoint} sparse supports is the easy case of the latter.
\textbf{Editing, memory, unlearning.} MEMIT \citep{memit} edits facts in place and can override; FSA adds paths that can
suppress or correct outputs while enabled but cannot edit a base weight; GRACE \citep{grace} and WISE \citep{wise} keep deletable, routed side-memory for sequential edits
while freezing the base; SISA \citep{sisa} is exact unlearning by shard isolation; TOFU \citep{tofu}
benchmarks fictitious-fact forgetting. \textbf{Personalization and retrieval.} OPPU \citep{oppu} attaches a
per-user PEFT module (removed by user identity, not per record); LaMP \citep{lamp} is a personalization
benchmark with retrieval baselines; RAG \citep{rag} and kNN-LM \citep{knnlm} store facts externally and
delete them by dropping a record---the baseline any ``delete a fact'' claim must beat.
\textbf{Low-bit models.} BitNet b1.58 \citep{bitnet158,bitnet2b4t} and LittleBit \citep{littlebit}. Bop \citep{bop} optimizes binary
weights without latent real weights; we extend its gradient-memory rule to ternary birth and death
(\S\ref{sec:writers}).

\section{Method}
\subsection{Format and reserve}
In \texttt{pair8}, weights form aligned blocks of eight (four pairs); each weight is ternary times a scale,
and at most two pairs per block are non-zero. A block thus has $417$ reachable states
($1 + 4\!\cdot\!8 + \binom{4}{2}8^2$), i.e. $\log_2 417/8 \approx 1.088$ bits/weight asymptotically
($1.125$ at a fixed $9$ bits/block), and is $\ge 50\%$ zero \emph{by construction} (ternary alone
guarantees no such fraction). \textbf{Writable reserve.} Zeros inside already-active pairs can be filled while preserving pair
occupancy and the codebook. At two active pairs, opening a third pair is illegal, but filling a zero
member of an active pair is legal. Thus the writable in-codebook reserve is a subset of the full zero
support, not necessarily empty in a full-occupancy block. With pair occupancy frozen, opening new pairs requires a separate sidecar patch outside the
base codebook. Filled positions and signs must still be stored
for revocation; the joint rate of 1.088 bits/weight is not the marginal price of a filled slot.

\subsection{Adaptation and exact revoke}
Train only the free-slot patch $\Delta W$, with support $\Rd\cap\Sz=\varnothing$, while the base
remains live. Base weights, per-row scales, pair occupancy, router, and norms are frozen. A legal
ternary writer fills free slots with $\pm\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the frozen row scale. The enabled
model uses $W+\Delta W$; zeroing the filled slots returns every patched tensor bit-identical to $W$,
since the patch never overwrites base support or shared state. In every run, patch-off logits were
bitwise equal to the base on one 512-token held-out window, and test recall returned to 0/80.

This restores the base, without guaranteeing its behavior while the patch is on. It does not delete
individual facts within a patch. If later sessions train with an earlier patch enabled, zeroing that
session's slots alone need not remove its influence on retained patches or training state
(\S\ref{sec:direction}).

\subsection{Honest accounting}
Plain ternary FSA adds \textbf{zero new logical parameters}: the element count is unchanged.
It fills only zeros inside active pairs, so a merged patch uses the same packed tensors and
adds no tensors or inference compute. This claim depends on preserving pair occupancy and the serving
format; opening new pairs or using a separate patch can add work. Synthetic legal patches have been
benchmarked in packed serving (\S\ref{sec:serving}); learned patches have not. Storage is not free: revocation needs positions and signs in a stored
diff, or the original pack. The learned-block-scale arm adds parameters and storage and is outside these packed-format claims. A full per-domain mask would cost one bit
per weight; sparse position coding avoids that cost for sparse patches. Frozen row scales constrain
each ternary slot to $\{-\alpha,0,+\alpha\}$, a capacity restriction. Actual position-inclusive patch
sizes are reported in \S\ref{sec:results}; hypothetical trit-only accounting is insufficient.

\section{Real-model setup}
\label{sec:real}
Lambda is an 8B-parameter ternary MoE with 128 routed experts per MoE
layer, 12 active per token, model width 1152, and a recurrent sequence-mixing state between layers.
Each routed expert has a $2304\!\times\!384$ up projection, squared ReLU, and a
$384\!\times\!2304$ down projection, stored as \texttt{pair8} trits times frozen per-row scales.
We patch layers 14--15, all 128 experts, both projections: a broad footprint with 66,807,179 free slots
inside already-active pairs. Base support, scales, pair occupancy, router, and all other parameters
remain frozen. Plain ternary births take value $\pm\alpha$ for row scale $\alpha$. The block-scale
arm keeps ternary signs but multiplies them by learned magnitudes, one per row and 16 input columns,
initialized at $0.5\alpha$. Its values leave $\{-\alpha,0,+\alpha\}$; the packed-format and
no-new-parameter claims do not cover this arm. The fp4 variant uses magnitudes
$\{0,0.125,0.25,0.375,0.5,0.75,1,1.5\}\alpha$ (the E2M1 grid scaled by $0.25\alpha$),
with minimum nonzero magnitude $0.125\alpha$ and cap $1.5\alpha$; it leaves the ternary codebook. Evaluation decodes experts to bf16. Legal ternary patches could use the same packed
kernel; synthetic patches have been benchmarked there (\S\ref{sec:serving}), while accuracy
evaluation still used decoded bf16 weights.

The task has 40 synthetic facts about a fictional person (favorite cheese, boat's name, house number).
Training lasts 300 steps with answer-token-only loss. Each step samples three phrasings per fact from
a pool of 12 templates (three plain sentences and nine augmented forms, including
``Question:/Answer:'' and ``User:/Assistant:''). Two further phrasings and $16\!\times\!512$ general-text
windows provide validation monitoring and checkpoint selection. Test recall is scored under teacher forcing: given the prompt, the model's greedy (argmax) prediction
must match every answer token. The new runs also measure free-running greedy generated recall:
the whole answer must match at a word boundary and a terminator or end-of-sequence must occur within
two tokens after it. Older runs test teacher-forced recall only. Recall is measured
on two phrasings never used in training or validation: ``Q: What is X's attr? A:'' and
``A little-known fact about X: her attr is''. The Q/A form is held out verbatim but resembles trained
question/answer formats. There are 80 test items; one item is 1.25 percentage points, and the unpatched
model gets 0/80. Damage is mean per-token $\Delta$NLL relative to the base on 40 disjoint 512-token
WikiText windows (about 20k tokens), with paired standard error (SE) over windows. The unpatched
model's NLL is 2.890 on a separate small diagnostic pool of five 512-token held-out windows,
which also provides the reference for the chaos-floor NLL ranges.
All results use one seed per run; same-configuration repeats do not test robustness across seeds.
Seeds pending for the headline configuration. Table~\ref{tab:real} reports step 300, except repair,
which adds 50 steps with births disabled.

\section{A chaos floor for KL}
Random, untrained ternary flips produce a KL floor while NLL on the separate five-window diagnostic
pool stays within about 0.01 nats of the unpatched model. For the down-projection flips in
Table~\ref{tab:chaos} and Figure~\ref{fig:chaos}, the range is about $-0.010$ to $+0.003$ relative to
the diagnostic-pool base NLL of 2.890; across all flip settings tested, it is about $-0.010$ to $+0.005$. A single layer-15 zero in one expert's down projection
illustrates the bf16 threshold: changes at most $0.01\alpha$ round away; a full trit changes about 6\%
of top-1 tokens. Two different single flips disagree with each other by KL 0.027, slightly more than one flip
differs from the base (0.024), and the divergence is roughly flat across sequence position. These observations suggest a different
but equally good trajectory after a change exceeds bf16 resolution. Near-ties in discrete MoE routing,
propagated through recurrent state, are a plausible driver, not a proven explanation.

\subsection{Honest reading}
At a comparable byte budget, the best FSA patch recalls 79/80 TF and 79/80 GEN from 0.33 MB
entropy-coded or 0.84 MB indexed. Across every LoRA rank and measured precision tried, ranks 1--4
recall 75--78/80 TF and 75--77/80 GEN at 0.69--11.01 MB (int4 to bf16). The nearest LoRA size is
rank 1 int4, 0.69 MB: 75/80 on both metrics versus 79/80 for FSA. The two FSA encodings bracket
that size; this is a comparable budget, not exact byte equality. The gap is 1--4 items on one seed:
suggestive, not established; seeds pending. FSA does not beat LoRA overall on this evidence.

The best penalized FSA runs, with or without rehearsal, and fp32 penalized LoRA all have
$|\Delta\mathrm{NLL}|\le0.003$ with SE around 0.002: at this sample size no damage difference is
detectable between them. This summary does not cover rank-1 bf16/int8 evaluations, whose measured
damage is $+0.0040/+0.0049$ (Table~\ref{tab:quant}). Plain Bop without the penalty costs
$+0.024$ to $+0.031$; those rows remain as history. Rehearsal uses the same corpus as the damage
panel, with no shared 32-token sequences, so near-zero damage is measured in-distribution;
other kinds of text are untested. Removing the patch restores the base exactly, without establishing
preservation while enabled. Multi-session concepts remain untested.

\section{Writers}
\label{sec:writers}
\subsection{Ternary Bop} Bop is Helwegen et al.'s binary optimizer \citep{bop}. Our ternary
extension adds the birth and death rules below and resets the gradient EMA after every change.
Each free slot holds
$q_j\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ directly, with no latent real weight. For loss gradient $g_j$, maintain
\[
m_j\leftarrow(1-\gamma)m_j+\gamma g_j,\qquad \gamma=0.05.
\]
If $q_j=0$ and $|m_j|>\tau$, birth sets $q_j\leftarrow-\operatorname{sign}(m_j)$.
If $q_j\ne0$, $|m_j|>\tau$, and $\operatorname{sign}(m_j)=q_j$, death sets $q_j\leftarrow0$:
the gradient says the current sign hurts. Reset $m_j$ to zero after each change. Thresholds are 0.7 or
0.9 times the RMS of the first answer-loss gradient over free slots. Historical plain Bop runs use
no penalty; new runs add the local penalty below. Bop does not use weight decay. The
threshold controls flips: small gradients need not become full $\pm\alpha$ steps, unlike Adam's
per-coordinate normalization, which can turn persistent small gradients into full flips. The
In the historical single-seed runs, slots are filled early: 812,077 slots were filled at step 50 (812,420 at step 300) at $\tau=0.7$. Higher $\tau$ uses fewer
slots (589k) at slightly higher damage, so it is not a simple damage dial. The $\tau=0.7$
repeat has 669,465 filled slots at step 50 and 669,705 at step 300; its revoke is also bit-exact,
with test recall returning to 0/80.

\subsection{Learn-then-prune} These historical comparisons use a single seed per run. Each free slot has an fp32 master $w_j$ in row-scale units. Forward uses
$q_j=\operatorname{sign}(w_j)\mathrm{I}(|w_j|>0.5)$, where $\mathrm{I}$ is the indicator; gradients pass straight through
\citep{ste}. AdamW \citep{adamw}, learning rate 0.02, applies decoupled weight decay 0.3 to masters.
The model learns early: at step 50 validation recall is 97.5\%, with 20.3M filled slots and validation
$\Delta$NLL $+2.2$. Decay pulls weakly supported masters under threshold: 3.2M slots at step 100,
1.1M at 150, and 0.54M at 300. Validation damage falls to about $+0.02$ and validation recall stays
100\%; test recall is separately 91.25\%. Held-out damage fell from $+0.0282$ at step 250 to
$+0.0250$ at step 300, while validation damage rose slightly ($+0.016 \to +0.018$).
With decay 0.5, filled slots fall from 14.18M at step 50 (98.75\% validation recall)
to 1.12M at step 100, 0.62M at 150, 0.50M at 200, and 0.45M at 250 (92.5\% validation recall),
then rise to 0.52M at 300 (97.5\% validation recall). Test recall at step 300 is 88.75\%;
revoke is bit-exact. Whether longer pruning helps is untested.
\par

\subsection{Fp4 and the local output penalty} Four-bit float slots times $\alpha$ use AdamW decay 0.1
and add the general-text penalty
\[
\lambda\frac{\sum_{l,t}\|x_t\Delta W_l^T\|^2}{\sum_{l,t}\|x_tW_l^T\|^2+10^{-12}},\qquad\lambda=10,
\]
where $l$ ranges over patched linear projections, $t$ over general-text tokens, $x_t$ is the input to
that projection, $W_l$ is its frozen base weight, and $\Delta W_l$ is the patch. The denominator,
base-weight output energy on the current inputs, is treated as a constant normalizer and is not
differentiated. This smooth local
penalty measures each patched projection's direct output change rather than the change in final
logits, so it is not dominated by the whole-model divergence behind the KL floor. Its inputs still come from the patched forward
pass: earlier patched layers can change later inputs and routing. The best LoRA run uses the same penalty
with $\lambda=100$. At that strength the penalty stopped earlier ternary learning: its gradient
is zero at zero, yet one birth costs a full $\alpha^2\mathbb{E}[x^2]$. Bop with $\lambda=10$
now learns while keeping measured damage within noise of zero (Table~\ref{tab:real}).
\par


\subsection{Budget and preservation variants}
\textbf{Gain-minus-cost budget.} At most 300k slots may be filled. Every 10 steps the allowed set
is re-chosen by each slot's gradient evidence minus its expected general-text cost,
$\alpha^2$ times the input column's general-text energy. Births go where evidence for the facts
is strongest relative to expected general-text change.
\textbf{Rehearsal.} The headline objective is $0.8$ times fact loss plus $0.2$ times ordinary
next-token loss on general WikiText windows, alongside the local penalty. Those windows share
no 32-token sequences with the damage panel but come from the same corpus. Near-zero damage is
therefore measured in-distribution; other kinds of text remain untested.
\textbf{Energy-scaled threshold.} The row's birth threshold is multiplied by
$\operatorname{clamp}((e_{\rm row}/\operatorname{median}e)^{0.5},0.25,4)$, where $e_{\rm row}$
is the expected general-text output energy of one birth in that row. Sensitive rows need more evidence.
\textbf{Repair.} After 300 training steps, 50 more Bop steps keep the local penalty at $\lambda=10$
and disable births, allowing deaths only. All variants use one seed; small recall differences do
not establish their relative value. Seeds pending.

\subsection{Ingredients that mattered} Answer-only loss avoids learning template boilerplate, which
caused most early damage with whole-sentence loss. Twelve diverse formats, including Q/A, largely
closed the validation-to-test gap (Bop and penalized LoRA test at their validation recall;
learn-then-prune decay 0.3 remains 100\% validation vs 91.25\% test). An earlier protocol reached
only 54\%/56\% held-out recall. The broad footprint mattered: 32 selected experts in one layer, down only, with a 500k-slot budget (350k--404k slots filled)
starved capacity (19--29\% recall). Damage uses the roughly 20k-token held-out pool, with checkpoint selection on a
separate validation pool.

\subsection{What did not work} KL penalties fight the chaos floor and cost recall. Ternary slots with
smooth penalty and decay peak in recall, then are pruned away. Tiny fp4 slots in late layer 28,
down only, with a 2M-slot budget and grid magnitudes starting at $0.03125\alpha$, fill about 2.0M
slots but yield 0\% recall at $+0.0067$ damage: the patch changed the model but learned none of
the tested answers. Patch-only learned block scales are no better than plain ternary
(87.5\%/$+0.028$) and add substantial storage. Single-late-layer continuous bf16 slots give
48.75\%/$+0.047$, versus same-placement LoRA's 37.5\%/$+0.0014$: a recall--damage trade, not an
overall improvement over LoRA. Gain-minus-cost ternary slot selection at that placement gives
60\%/$+0.064$.

\section{Toward epigenesis: multi-session learning}
\label{sec:direction}
The ternary reserve now carries facts on a real model (\S\ref{sec:real}). A revocable domain patch
is therefore demonstrated for this task: enable the slice for its domain; disable it to recover the base.
The personal-model application remains an untested proposal, with a narrower contract:
\par

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Patch-revocation contract.} Deleting an isolated patch exactly removes \emph{that patch's parameter
contribution}. Equivalence to a model trained without its data additionally requires that no retained patch
or training state depended on it. It does not delete individual facts (facts within a slice are
superimposed), and it does not make information already in the base inaccessible.
\end{quote}

Retrieval deletes individual records while leaving the base unchanged \citep{rag,knnlm}; disabling a
per-user adapter removes that user's contribution \citep{oppu}. The case for weights is skills, style,
and policy that benefit from adaptation in every forward pass. Arbitrary facts remain a retrieval
baseline's natural task. The real ternary fact experiment offers smaller patches at greater damage, not a general
personalization result.

The pre-registered EP-1 experiment is in progress and explicitly \textbf{untested}: five sessions with
a synthetic user containing 40 policies, 100 recurrent facts (each in 3 of 5 sessions), and 100
one-off ``lure'' facts (20 per session), consolidating self-study into free ternary slots. An identifiability gate requires the in-context ceiling to beat
the unpatched model by at least 10 concept accuracy points on development probes; otherwise nothing is scored.
Success criteria fixed in advance require, after session 5, concept gain of at least 10 concept accuracy
points over the unpatched model on held-out probes, lure recall no more than 5 accuracy points above it, and
mean general-text $\Delta$NLL at most $+0.01$ nats/token on a locked $40\!\times\!512$ WikiText panel,
disjoint from the selection pool, with a paired 95\% damage interval excluding $+0.05$. Final concept accuracy must retain at least 80\% of the best earlier session's accuracy,
with no upward ratchet in lure recall (monotone non-decreasing with a net increase).

The pre-registered ternary arm (``ternary with block scales'') uses a 2M-slot ceiling and
learned block scales initialized at $0.25\alpha$, so like the block-scale arm here it is outside the
packed-format / no-new-parameter claims. A separate fp4 arm is also registered; the comparator is
regularized LoRA at the same placement.

The pre-registered revoke criterion clears the patch, scales, optimizer and training state, replay,
and session ledger. It requires every patched weight tensor to be bitwise equal to the
base and format-legal, logits on 32 fixed prompts within $10^{-2}$ of the base, and concept/lure
metrics back to the base within noise. Exact logit equality is reported separately; it was observed
when switching patches off in the fact experiment (\S\ref{sec:real}). ``Match LoRA'' means within 3 concept accuracy points and no more than $+0.005$
extra damage, with the lure constraint held and revoke passing; ``beat LoRA'' means at least 5 more
concept accuracy points with damage no worse than LoRA. Per the pre-registration, one seed is a pilot; three
seeds are needed to claim. Neither multi-session concept learning nor on-device
training is established: optimizer state can be large even when a final patch is small. A broader
evaluation should include local retrieval and per-user adapters on personalization benchmarks
\citep{lamp}, with unlearning measured \citep{sisa,tofu}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Damage and recall.} The best penalized FSA runs and fp32 penalized LoRA are within noise of
zero on 40 WikiText windows, without a detectable damage difference at this sample size.
Plain Bop costs $+0.024$ to $+0.031$. Damage on other corpora and broader behavior is untested;
rehearsal windows are sequence-disjoint but drawn from the same corpus as the damage panel.
Generated recall is now measured for the new runs, checking unaided answer production and a clean
stop; older runs remain teacher-forced only.

\textbf{Evaluation scope.} We have one task, 40 facts about one fictional person, 80 test items,
and one seed per run. Same-configuration repeats vary by a couple of items and are not evidence of
robustness across seeds. A 1--4-item recall gap is suggestive, not established; seeds pending for the
headline configuration. The Q/A test form resembles training; writer and regularization choices
differ. The chaos-floor explanation is a hypothesis. The roughly 20k-token damage pool cannot
establish broad behavioral safety. Multi-session concept learning remains untested in EP-1.

\textbf{Revoke scope.} Removing the whole patch restores the base bit-for-bit. This does not guarantee
preservation while the patch is on, deletion of individual facts, removal of information already in
the base, or removal of an earlier session's influence after later sessions trained on top of it.
The frozen base can be augmented or suppressed through new paths but its existing weights cannot
be refined.

\textbf{Format and deployment.} Plain ternary writers stay legal pair8, but evaluation decoded experts
to bf16; packed-kernel serving is measured for synthetic legal patches only, with learned patches
not yet benchmarked. Fp4 and learned-block-scale variants leave the
original codebook. Optimizer state can be large even when the final patch is small, and on-device
training is not established.

\textbf{Privacy.} Knowledge in weights can be elicited. A per-user model is the proposed deployment for the personal application; its safety is untested, and
the patch mechanism supplies no privacy guarantee.

\section{Conclusion}
A frozen weight genome can acquire a removable sparse layer in its exact zeros. On a real 8B ternary
MoE, Bop with a local penalty, a slot budget and rehearsal recalls 79/80 teacher-forced and generated
items from 249,755 slots (0.33--0.84 MB including positions) at $-0.0001\pm0.0017$ damage.
The nearest-size LoRA, rank 1 int4 at 0.69 MB, recalls 75/80 on both metrics. Across tested ranks
and precisions, a 1--4-item gap on one seed is suggestive, not established; seeds pending.
No damage difference is detectable between the best penalized FSA runs and fp32 penalized LoRA
at this sample size, on the same corpus used for rehearsal. FSA does not beat LoRA overall on this
evidence. Synthetic packed patches have no detectable serving cost and revoke byte-identically;
learned-patch serving, broader preservation and multi-session concept learning remain untested.
The pre-registered EP-1 experiment is in progress.
